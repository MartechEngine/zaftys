# FASTag, barrier-free tolling, and GNSS: what Indian freight should plan for

**Status:** Implemented locally, 8 Oct 2026. Not deployed.  
**Voice:** Plain plant-desk English. No em dash or en dash. Spaced hyphens only.  
**Honesty:** Separate what is operating, what is only notified, and what is still a 2024 design. Do not turn a workshop rupee into an NHAI tariff.

## Decision

Do not publish the supplied draft as written. The topic is right. The operating claim is not.

On 8 Oct 2026 the collection rail for a commercial truck is still **FASTag**. Barrier-free **multi-lane free flow (MLFF)** is live at a short named list of plazas, and it still debits FASTag. It does not calculate a satellite kilometre. GNSS distance billing was written into the National Highways Fee Rules in September 2024, then held back. In July 2026 the government told Parliament there is no timeline to replace plazas with satellite tolling, because expert committees want more work on security, privacy, breach, and operational control.

The useful post teaches a freight desk three jobs:

1. Audit the FASTag line that is already on the bill.
2. Know what changes when a plaza on the lane becomes MLFF, and what does not.
3. Read the GNSS rule so a contract is not written as if pay-per-kilometre were already national law.

### What we will not claim

- GNSS is not the live national toll system.
- MLFF is not pay-per-kilometre. The boom can go. The section fee can stay.
- The car annual pass does not apply to trucks.
- AIS-140 plus NavIC is a 2024 design for a future on-board unit. It is not a current order to fit every trailer.
- Do not use 12% to 18% of variable cost, 15 to 25 minutes saved per 500 km, 0.35 to 0.50 litres per plaza stop, or a Delhi to Mumbai rupee total. Those figures were not in the official sources checked for this brief.
- Do not promise 100% automated toll reconciliation. A TMS can store the match. It cannot invent a gantry feed that the lane does not have.

### B. Barrier-free plazas, still on FASTag

MLFF here means a gantry with RFID readers and ANPR cameras. The vehicle is not required to stop, slow down, or stay in one lane. The debit is still FASTag. A low balance or a dead tag can become an electronic notice.

The 30 July 2026 reply said 17 plazas had been awarded and five were live: Choryasi (NH-48, Gujarat), Mundka (UER-II, Delhi), Gharaunda (NH-44, Haryana), Manoharpura (NH-48, Rajasthan), and Daulatpura (NH-48, Rajasthan). Another 104 had been identified.

The 1 Oct 2026 PIB release (2317639) then names a wider live set: Chorayasi, Mundka, Shahjahanpur, Daulatpura, Manoharpura, Gharaunda, and Paranur on the Tambaram to Tindivanam section. The July annex had listed Paranur against NH-45. The October release says NH-179B. Cite both dates rather than silently picking one highway number.

This is a phased list, not a national switch-off of booms. Re-read the NHAI or PIB note before naming a plaza as live in the published article.

### C. The GNSS rule that is not yet the bill

The National Highways Fee (Determination of Rates and Collection) Amendment Rules, 2024, reported as notified on 10 September 2024 and listed in later amendment chains as G.S.R. 556(E) dated 9 September 2024, added GNSS electronic toll collection to the 2008 rules. Confirm that gazette PDF before a contract quotes it. From the ministry text as reported:

- A mechanical vehicle that is **not** a national permit vehicle gets zero user fee for up to 20 km in each direction in a day on the same section, under a GNSS user-fee system.
- Above that, the fee follows the actual distance travelled.
- A lane may be earmarked for a vehicle with a GNSS on-board unit. A vehicle that enters it without a valid, functional unit pays two times the user fee at that plaza.
- The unit is non-transferable and firmly fitted.
- NHAI's 2024 plan was a hybrid: FASTag lanes and GNSS lanes together, then a later conversion. By July 2026 the government had chosen MLFF on FASTag instead of a GNSS timetable.

Indian Express reported on 10 September 2024 that the IHMCL tender for free-flow GNSS lanes had not been finalised. Do not describe that 2024 tender as a live procurement in this post.

### D. What the 2024 hardware paper actually said

IHMCL's June 2024 GNSS concept and expression of interest said a fully compliant AIS-140 vehicle-location device would be the proposed on-board unit. The toll charger would take anonymised time and location pings, with a virtual identity, and discard data that is not on the tolled highway. NavIC support sits in the wider AIS-140 device discussion as a proposed specification, not as a 2026 fitment order for goods vehicles.

Write that as design intent. Do not write "all heavy trucks must now carry a NavIC OBU."

### E. The only cost band this site should reuse

Earlier ZAFTYS workshop pages used a variable band of about ₹26 to ₹37 per km, of which toll was about ₹3 to ₹7 per km. Toll is then about 8% to 27% of that variable band. A midpoint of ₹5 toll inside ₹32 variable is about 16%. That is a teaching slice. It is not an NHAI measurement, and it is not "12% to 18% of every truck."

Diesel stays in the fuel clause. Toll stays on its own line. The diesel post already says this. Link it. Do not rebuild the fuel maths here.

## The maths the post should teach

Use one teaching rate so the shapes can be compared. It is not a 2026 multi-axle tariff.

```text
R = ₹4.50 per km
L = 60 km notified section
Section fee = R x L = ₹270
```

Under today's open plaza, and under MLFF at that same plaza, a truck that uses 4 km of the section and a truck that uses all 60 km can both be billed ₹270. The gantry removes the stop. It does not, by itself, shrink the section.

Use the GNSS formula only with this label: "if this section is actually operating as a GNSS section."

```text
National permit vehicle:
  chargeable km = actual km
  fee = R x actual km

Any other mechanical vehicle:
  chargeable km = max(0, actual km - 20)
  fee = R x chargeable km
```

The 20 km is per direction, per day, on the same section. It is not a national daily free slab, and it is not available to a national permit vehicle.

| Trip inside the 60 km section | Open plaza or MLFF | GNSS, national permit | GNSS, other mechanical vehicle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 km | ₹270 | ₹18 | ₹0 |
| 25 km | ₹270 | ₹112.50 | ₹22.50 |
| 60 km | ₹270 | ₹270 | ₹180 |

The gap that matters for industrial FTL is the first column against the second. Most interstate plant trucks are national permit vehicles, so the 20 km waiver is the wrong prize to sell them. The prize, when GNSS is actually switched on, is paying for 4 km instead of 60.

A closed expressway that already charges entry to exit is a different product from an open plaza. Say that in one paragraph. Do not force the Delhi to Mumbai Expressway into the ₹270 example.

If a vehicle without a valid GNSS unit enters an earmarked GNSS lane, the reported rule is two times the user fee at that plaza. On this workshop section that is ₹540, not a new criminal fine. Do not invent a blacklist penalty for a disconnected tracker. The live failure mode in October 2026 is a FASTag with no balance, a dead tag, or a wrong vehicle class, followed by an electronic notice at an MLFF gantry.

## What each chapter should contain

### 1. Three toll systems, and only one of them is the national default

Open on the Parliament sentence, not on a satellite future. One status board:

| System | October 2026 state | What the truck pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FASTag at a barrier plaza | National default | The notified fee for that plaza and vehicle class |
| MLFF gantry | Live at the named plazas, awarded at 17, identified at 104 more | The same FASTag fee, without a required stop |
| GNSS distance billing | Rule notified in 2024. No replacement timeline | Actual kilometres, only if that section is operating as GNSS |

Say, once, that the annual pass is a car product. Then leave it.

### 2. Why a four kilometre hop can still pay for sixty

Explain rule 8(2) in ordinary words. A plaza stands for a notified stretch. The stretch is normally at least 60 km before the next plaza in that direction, with a written exception. That is why a short diversion can look overcharged. It is also why "pay only for the kilometres you drove" is a real change, and why it is not the same change as removing the boom.

No dwell-time claim. No litres per stop.

### 3. The GNSS rule that is written, and who the 20 km waiver misses

Put the formula card and the worked table here. State the national permit exclusion in the first screen of the chapter. State that counsel should read the gazette before anyone pastes "20 km free" into a rate card.

### 4. What a barrier-free plaza changes on the road

PIB's own words are enough: the vehicle need not stop, slow down, or hold a lane, and the aim is less congestion, fuel use, and emissions. Do not convert that into minutes or litres.

What the desk must do instead:

- the FASTag has to be the right vehicle class
- the balance has to survive the whole lane, because there may be no boom and no cash window
- a failed read becomes an electronic notice, not a story at the next dhaba
- the same trip can still mix barrier plazas and MLFF gantries

e-Way Bill validity does not get a new distance rule because a gantry let the truck keep its speed. Link the existing e-Way guide. Do not invent a shorter expiry.

### 5. The on-board unit that was designed, and not yet ordered

One plain stack, labelled 2024 IHMCL design:

1. An AIS-140 vehicle-location device is the proposed unit.
2. It would send anonymised time and location pings, tied to a virtual identity and the vehicle class.
3. The toll charger would ignore pings that are not on the tolled highway.
4. The issuer would map the unit to the existing FASTag.
5. A GNSS lane would still need cameras, because a unit can fail and a non-equipped truck can enter it.

Then the decision: do not buy a special toll OBU for the whole fleet because a 2024 paper described one. Do buy a clean FASTag inventory, the right class on each vehicle, and a way to see a failed debit the same day.

### 6. How the toll line should look on a freight bill

The toll rupee is not part of the diesel percent. On the bill, finance should be able to see:

| Line | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Plaza or gantry name | FASTag statement or acquirer record |
| Time | Same statement |
| Vehicle number and class | Tag, RC, and the trip |
| Amount | Issuer debit, not a round number in WhatsApp |
| Trip | The loading and delivery record that was on that road |

A three-way toll match is statement, vehicle class, and trip. It is not a fourth copy of the ePOD match. If the class on the tag is a truck and the trip was a tractor-trailer, the debit can be wrong even when the gantry worked.

[ZAFTYS TMS](/zaftys-tms) is the place to keep that match on the trip. Say it as an operating choice. Do not say every plaza in India already pushes a live gantry feed into the product.

### 7. Twenty checks: do now, wait, and do not sign

Ten checks for the live FASTag and MLFF lane. Ten for a future GNSS clause. The second ten should fail closed: if the section is not a notified operating GNSS section, those lines stay out of the rate card.

## FAQ to draft for schema

**Is GNSS satellite tolling live on Indian highways?**  
No. In July 2026 the government said expert committees had asked for more deliberation, and that there was no timeline to replace plazas with satellite tolling. The live barrier-free project is MLFF, and it still uses FASTag.

**Does a barrier-free MLFF plaza charge by the kilometre?**  
No. It is built so the vehicle need not stop. The user fee is still the FASTag fee for that plaza and vehicle class.

**Does the 20 km free rule apply to trucks?**  
Only inside a GNSS user-fee system, and not to a national permit vehicle. It is up to 20 km in each direction in a day on the same section. It is not a live discount on today's plaza bills.

**Will FASTag stop working?**  
Not on the evidence available on 8 Oct 2026. GNSS was designed as a hybrid beside FASTag. The system now being rolled out still debits FASTag.

**Does the ₹3,075 annual pass cover a commercial trailer?**  
No. It is for non-commercial cars, jeeps, and vans.

**What should a freight bill show for toll?**  
The plaza or gantry, the time, the vehicle class, the debit, and the trip. A round monthly toll allowance is not an audit.

**What happens if the tag has no balance at an MLFF gantry?**  
The vehicle may pass, and the missed debit can become an electronic notice. Low balance is an operational failure, not a small delay at a window.

**Should we fit AIS-140 NavIC units now for satellite tolls?**  
Not because of this rule. The 2024 design pointed at an AIS-140 device. The government has not set a fitment deadline for goods vehicles.
