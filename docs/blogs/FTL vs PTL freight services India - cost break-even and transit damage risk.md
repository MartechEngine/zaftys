## 1. The fundamental fork in Indian freight: direct transit vs network consolidation

Indian industrial shippers face one structural choice on road freight:

**PTL (Part Truckload / Less-than-Truckload / sundry / parcel)**  
Many small consignments from different shippers consolidated into regional transshipment hubs (sundry godowns in Transport Nagars), then trunked and de-consolidated for last-mile delivery.

**FTL (Full Truckload)**  
A dedicated commercial vehicle contracted for a single consignor, moving point-to-point from origin dock to destination gate under one seal, one driver, one Part B.

### Structural profile of the Indian PTL market

- Regional sundry operators vs national parcel carriers.
- Multi-tiered commission booking agents (**dalal**) who book dockets, not own the trunk asset.
- Local branch godowns, regional transshipment centres (RTCs), and Out of Delivery Area (ODA) last-mile vans.

### Why growing manufacturers outgrow the parcel network

Sporadic component samples fit PTL. Steady factory production dispatches - palletised finished goods, machinery crates, painted panels, copper and electronics - outgrow sundry handling. Volume, fragility, and LD penalties push the desk toward dedicated FTL. For capacity model (contract vs spot FTL), see [spot market vs dedicated fleets](/blog/spot-market-vs-dedicated-fleet-india).

## 2. The physical journey: how goods move through PTL networks vs dedicated FTL

### PTL hub-and-spoke odyssey (typical 7 stages)

1. Local feeder pickup (tempo / LCV to local branch godown).
2. Local godown staging and inward sorting.
3. Regional Transshipment Center (RTC) consolidation onto multi-axle trunk trailer.
4. Line-haul between regional hubs.
5. Destination RTC inward sorting and de-consolidation.
6. Destination local branch transfer.
7. Last-mile Out-of-Delivery-Area (ODA) delivery.

### Handling friction (4 to 7 touches)

Manual labour dumping, hooks on bulk bags, forklift mast punctures, open rain-yard staging, cargo tumbling inside unlashed mixed loads. This is the physical root of elevated PTL damage bands. Damage claim mechanics stay in [transit damage and shortage claims](/blog/transit-damage-shortage-cargo-insurance-india-carrier-liability-guide).

### Point-to-point FTL

Factory dock loading -> driver physical verification -> tamper-evident bolt / electronic seal -> direct highway cruise -> unbroken seal audit at customer gate. Exactly one load and one unload touch for the consignor's cargo.

## 4. The volumetric weight trap (cube-out vs weigh-out in PTL)

Parcel networks bill **chargeable weight** = max(actual gross mass, volumetric weight).

### Standard Indian PTL conversions (confirm carrier tariff)

- **10 lbs per Cubic Foot (CFT)**, or
- **1 CBM = 250 to 300 kg** (carrier schedule dependent).

### Chargeable weight (workshop form - render as formula board, not raw LaTeX)

```text
Chargeable Weight =
  max(
    Actual Gross Mass,
    (L x W x H in inches) / 1728  x  10 lbs/CFT  x  0.4536 kg/lb
  )
```

### Density penalty

High-cube, lightweight engineering goods (control panels, hollow fabrications, plastics, packaging, air ducting) pay for "ghost weight." Effective freight can run **2x to 3x** the nominal ₹/kg on actual mass.

**EXHIBIT - Formula Board 2 [CUBE-OUT PENALTY]:**  
Example workshop: 2,200 kg actual of control panels at 12 CBM (~424 CFT) billed near **4,240 kg** volumetric under 10 lbs/CFT - roughly double the mass-based freight.

## 5. Mathematical modeling: the FTL vs PTL break-even equation

### Total Cost of Transshipment (workshop)

```text
Cost_PTL = (Weight x R_kg) + Surcharges + C_packaging + C_damage_scrap + C_inventory_drag

Cost_FTL = Trip Rate_dedicated + C_handling_dock
```

### Break-even weight (workshop algebraic form)

```text
W_breakeven =
  ( Trip Rate_FTL - [Docket + ODA + C_crating] )
  /
  ( (R_PTL/kg + FOV_rate x P_unit) - Delta_C_damage )
```

Render as **Formula Board 1 [MODAL CONVERSION THRESHOLD]** - clean card, no `$$` LaTeX in the live React page.

### Hidden break-even drift

Pure paper freight (ignore packaging + scrap) can show break-even near **~6.5 MT**. Adding secondary crating and damage scrap often pulls real break-even down to about **3.0 to 4.5 MT** on a standard ~1,000 km industrial corridor (workshop). Always recompute with your tariff and damage history.

## 7. Statutory and documentation compliance: e-Way Bill Part B multi-vehicle updating

### CGST Rule 138 challenges in PTL

- **Transshipment rule:** update Part B and, where applicable, multi-vehicle consolidated e-Way Bills (**Form GST EWB-02**) when cargo moves feeder tempo -> trunk trailer -> local delivery van.
- **Highway interception risk:** flying squads under **Section 129** can seize a sundry trailer because one docket among dozens has an expired bill or missing transshipment entry - trapping compliant cargo for days.

Confirm bare CGST Rules and CBIC circulars before a legal memo.

### FTL regulatory advantage

Clean single-vehicle Part B linkage. Uninterrupted point-to-point transit. Zero intermediate transshipment exposure for that consignment.

## 8. Worked numerical case study: 4.5-tonne industrial consignment

**Workshop corridor (not a ZAFTYS audited claim file):**  
Precision automotive machinery and electrical components, **4,500 kg (4.5 MT)**, 12 pallets, invoice **Rs 28,00,000**, Chakan (Pune) to Sanand (Ahmedabad), ~**660 km**.

| Model | Description |
| --- | --- |
| A | Leading national PTL sundry network - base **Rs 7.50/kg** |
| B | Dedicated 19-ft closed-body FTL (~6.5 T payload) - **Rs 38,000** trip via ZAFTYS desk framing |

### Line-item reconciliation (workshop)

| Line | PTL Model A | FTL Model B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base freight | Rs 33,750 (4,500 x 7.50) | Rs 38,000 |
| Docket + hamali + FOV | + Rs 4,740 | in trip / dock handling |
| Mandatory wooden crating / corners | + Rs 9,000 | stripped / minimal |
| Working capital drag (5-day delta @ 12% WACC on Rs 28 L) | + Rs 550 | baseline |
| Damage scrap / rejection allowance (1.8% PTL vs ~0% FTL) | + Rs 5,040 | - |
| Transit time | ~6 days | ~22 hours |
| **True landed** | **Rs 53,490** | **Rs 38,000** |

**Reveal:** PTL looked cheaper at Rs 33,750 sticker. Landed PTL **Rs 53,490**. Dedicated FTL **Rs 38,000** saves about **Rs 15,490 (~29%)** and cuts ~5 days.

## 9. Strategic decision framework: when to stay on PTL vs when to switch to FTL

### 2-axis matrix: Shipment density / volume vs Cargo value / fragility

**Stay on PTL when:**

- Sub-~2 tonne shipments of non-fragile, low-value, high-density industrial spares.
- Sporadic samples to remote Tier-3/4 non-industrial locations.
- Origin plant cannot stage a full truckload at the dock.

**Switch to FTL when:**

- Consolidated customer dispatches above ~**3.5 tonnes**.
- Finished goods vulnerable to water, moisture, or surface scratch (machinery, CR steel, painted panels, chemicals).
- Time-critical line-feeding with Late Delivery (LD) penalties.
- High-value pilferage-prone cargo (copper, electronics, brass, automotive batteries).

## 10. Frequently asked questions and statutory citations

### FAQs (schema priorities)

1. **Can we book dedicated FTL if cargo only fills half the truck?**  
   Yes. That is dead-freight trade-off vs risk elimination. Compare dead-freight rupees to PTL packaging + damage + inventory drag on the same lane.

2. **How does the Carriage by Road Act 2007 treat liability on PTL parcel receipts vs dedicated FTL LRs?**  
   Common-carrier liability (Sections 9-11, Rule 12) still turns on registration, goods receipt, and declared value. PTL multi-party custody and clean dockets often weaken evidence. Dedicated FTL with seal + qualified remarks is cleaner for surveyors. Detail: [transit damage guide](/blog/transit-damage-shortage-cargo-insurance-india-carrier-liability-guide).

3. **What is the standard ODA distance threshold in Indian PTL?**  
   Carrier-tariff specific. Many networks flag docks outside primary municipal / pin-code service lists. Confirm the tariff annex - do not invent a national km rule.

4. **How should a plant move from daily small PTL to consolidated bi-weekly FTL?**  
   Run SKU density audit, set consolidation rhythm, strip over-crating, size the body class, lock route and seals, close with ePOD. Use the 8-point protocol above.

### Statutory citations (confirm bare text)

- **CGST Act, 2017** - Rule 138, 138A; Form GST EWB-02 (transshipment / multi-vehicle e-Way Bills).
- **Carriage by Road Act, 2007** and **Rules, 2011** - Sections 9, 10; Rule 12 (common carrier liability limits).
- **Indian Contract Act, 1872** - Bailment Sections 148-161 (orientation for custody; counsel owns litigation strategy).
